## Introduction

### What is a Census Block, Precinct, & District?

#### **Census Block**

A census block is the smallest unit of geography used by the U.S. Census Bureau. Think of it like the building block of all other areas.

#### **Precinct**

A precinct is the smallest political unit where elections are administered. It’s where you’re assigned to go vote. Multiple precincts make up a district

#### District 

A **district** is a large geographic area used to elect a representative. Think of it as a voting zone for a specific level of government.

### What is a District Map?

A district map is a map that shows how a geographic area—like a state, county, or city—is divided into political districts. These districts determine which voters are grouped together for the purpose of electing representatives to legislative bodies (like Congress, state legislatures, or city councils).

A quick look at the **Arizona Congressional District Map**

- Is used to elect U.S. House of Representatives members (Arizona has 9 congressional districts as of the 2020 Census).
- Each congressional district elects one representative to Congress.
- These districts must have roughly equal populations, based on total population from the Census.

A quick look at the **Arizona State Legislative District Map**

- Is used to elect State Senator and State House Representative in elections, ensuring fair and equal representation across the state. (Arizona has 30 State Senate districts as of the 2020 Census).
- Each state legislative district elects one senator and two state house representatives.
- As of the 2020 Census, each of Arizona's 30 legislative districts has a population of about 230,000 residents.

### What is Redistricting

Redistricting is the process of redrawing the boundaries of electoral districts — the geographic areas used to elect representatives to legislative bodies like Congress, state legislatures, city councils, etc.

Redistricting usually happens every 10 years after the census to reflect changes in population. The goal is to ensure equal representation, meaning each district should have roughly the same number of people.

### What is Gerrymandering

Is when the process of redistricting is manipulated to give an unfair advantage to a particular political party, group, or incumbent.

It usually results in oddly-shaped districts that don’t make geographic or community sense. Instead, they’re drawn to:

- **Pack** opponents into a few districts (so they win fewer overall)
- **Crack** opponents across many districts (so they don't form a majority anywhere)

## State Background

Politically Arizona is considered to be a purple swing state, where elections tend to be rather close.
However, most election outcomes historically lean republican. (270toWin, www.270towin.com/states/arizona. Accessed 20 Apr. 2025)

There was a 40% increase in population in the state of Arizona from 1990 to 2000's (U.S. Census Bureau. 2001). A significant change in a state's population
will require additional congressional and state legislative district boundaries to be drawn. Such a population change is also a potential indicator for a population socio-economic demographics
to shift as well as political ones.

Prior to 2001 the Arizona State Legislature was responsible for drawing the state's congressional and legislative district maps. This process was
controlled by the majority party in the legislature. Legislators were reluctant to drastically change district boundaries from which they found support in
and main goal was to protect incumbents.

The 1990's in Arizona 

In November 2000, Arizona voters passed Proposition 106, a citizen initiative that amended the Arizona Constitution by removing the power to draw congressional and state legislative districts from the state legislature and reassigning this task to the Independent Redistricting Commission (IRC)
(_AIRC_, https://irc.az.gov/about/proposition-106. Accessed 20 Apr. 2025.).
